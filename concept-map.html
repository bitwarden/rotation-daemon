Bitwarden Credential Rotation

Concept Map

Two specifications, one system. The Bitwarden server decides what & when; the rotation daemon does the how. They meet at a narrow API.

Server: rotation-server.allium Daemon: rotation-daemon.allium

1The big picture

An admin configures rotations on the server. The daemon does the work in the target system. Everything between server and daemon is the API.

flowchart LR Admin(["Administrator"]) -->|configures| Server["Bitwarden server"] Server -->|offers & authorises work| Daemon["Rotation daemon"] Daemon -->|claims · writes back · reports| Server Daemon -->|rotates credential| Target(["Target system"])
The two arrows between server and daemon are the API. Section 3 lists exactly what crosses.

2The rotation lifecycle

One rotation, end to end. A server-side job spawns a daemon-side attempt; the attempt writes the new password and reports back.

flowchart LR Config["RotationConfig"] -->|comes due| Job["RotationJob
server"] Job -->|daemon claims| Attempt["RotationAttempt
daemon"] Attempt -->|writes new password| Cipher[("Cipher")] Attempt -.->|reports outcome| Job
The RotationJob ↔ RotationAttempt pair is the spine: the job is durable and counts attempts; the attempt is one try. A failed attempt makes the job claimable again.

4The shape it reveals

Ownership is one-directional
The server owns what & when (TargetSystem, PasswordPolicy, RotationConfig, RotationJob, eligibility). The daemon owns how (connection details resolved locally by id, integration execution, encryption). The daemon never references a server type — it works entirely from the claim snapshot.
The job-attempt pair is the spine
A RotationJob (server-side, durable, counts attempts, recovers) spawns a RotationAttempt (daemon-side, one try). They are correlated not by a back-reference but through Cipher → RotationConfig → the config’s single claimed job — which is why OneConfigPerCipher and AtMostOneActiveJobPerConfig matter.
The job doubles as the auth token
Cipher writes are authorised against the claimed job’s ownership (job.claimed_by) plus its in-flight status — so dispatch and write-authorisation are the same concept. That is exactly why a dropped, released, or timed-out job auto-fences stale writes (RejectCipherUpdate): there is no longer a claimed job for this writer.
